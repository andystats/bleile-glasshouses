The color describes what you can do in this scenario, not an immutable type of bias. Blue light effects stand for the specific repair named on screen. Finding a bug never creates one or worsens the study.

About the score

Each bug is one stipulated concern. Its size follows the remaining 0–10 concern score for its component. Hover to see the study-specific reason. The total includes bugs still hidden in the dark. Revealing one changes what you have found, not the study’s condition.

The observational example starts at 24. Recovering pretreatment information clears three confounding Bleile points, measurement validation clears one, and a specified reassuring sensitivity assessment clears one. The total reaches 19. Positivity stays at 4. The available repairs cannot supply the missing comparison.

The randomized, observational and poorly specified houses are fictional examples, not automatic scores for those study types. A zero would represent an idealized assessment, not guaranteed truth. The score is not measured bias, a probability, a validated index, or a universal ranking. No treatment effect is estimated.

Some concerns require better data, stronger assumptions or a different question. A changed question needs its own assessment.

Bleile Glasshouses

Each glasshouse is a study. Take a light inside and find what could spoil its causal claim.

✳✳✳

A study claims that a treatment caused an outcome.

Every bug in its glasshouse is one reason to doubt that claim, such as a confounder nobody measured or patients who never had a real alternative. One bug is one Bleile point. Most of them start in the dark.

Your light is investigation.

Sweep it through the glasshouse and the bugs show themselves. Finding one never makes the study worse.

A blue bug can be cleared: this study has the information or the check that repairs it. A coral bug stays. Nothing available in this study removes it.

With predictions on, each bug in the records house stays pale until you say which of the two it will be.

    Each bug belongs to one step of the causal roadmap. Clearing one step does nothing for another: measuring more confounders cannot supply a comparison that was never there.
